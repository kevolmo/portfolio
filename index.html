Leadership through data

From knowledge silos to a self-sustaining team.

I inherited a team where information was often held by individuals, roles were divided by task and business line, and people hesitated to speak openly about mistakes. The transformation began by making the work—and the knowledge behind it—shared.

BeforeFragmented ownership

Knowledge silos, narrow task boundaries, separate business-line thinking, and limited visibility.

→
BuildShared operating system

Cross-training, common KPIs, standups, job aids, workflows, and visible process controls.

→
TodayDistributed ownership

A team that redirects to business need, shares lessons, acts independently, and escalates exceptions.

01All 14 team members became cross-functional

What began as one person per designated area became full-team coverage across repairs, ticketing, repair-order and distribution picking, urgent demand, and workplace controls.

02Standups began producing solutions

A unit-of-measure discussion prompted an associate to share a calculation spreadsheet with the entire team—turning one lesson into reusable standard work.

03Capability created mobility

Team members progressed into Master Scheduling, Operations Specialist, higher-level roles, and a developing lead pipeline.

Adoption signal · Push became pull

Trust was earned by making the work easier.

Dashboards, SharePoint, SAP helpers, and Power Apps initially met skepticism. Consistent delivery changed the conversation: users began requesting features, forklift requests became part of daily work, and another team adopted a similar SharePoint pattern. The tools moved from something introduced to the team to something the team helped improve.
