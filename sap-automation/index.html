SAP automation · Workflow design

One guided scan flow replaced a fragile chain of SAP transactions.

A human-centered helper simplified repair-station issuing, preserved the decisions that require operator judgment, and automated the repetitive steps surrounding them.

<10sestimated guided processing per line without errors
~400repair-station lines processed during a post-outage recovery day
~100orders reached by roughly noon–1 p.m. instead of near day-end

The operating constraint

The work was not difficult because of one transaction. It was difficult because of the handoffs between them.

For every repair order, an associate moved across MIGO, LT06, and LT12 while tracking batches, quantities, serial profiles, line confirmations, bins, and transfer orders. An interruption could force the person to reconstruct where they were in the process.

The design goal was not automation for its own sake. It was to reduce memory load, prevent skipped steps, and let associates keep their attention on accuracy and flow.

Workflow redesign

From transaction navigation to guided execution.

The interface reorganized the work around what the associate scans—not around the order of SAP screens.

BeforeManual, screen-led flow
  1. 01
    MIGO

    Find and sort matching order lines, part numbers, and batches.

  2. 02
    Serial validation

    Check each line for serial-number requirements and confirm line status.

  3. 03
    LT06

    Create the transfer order and collect the stock-placement details.

  4. 04
    LT12

    Confirm the transfer order after the movement is created.

AfterGuided, scan-led flow
  1. 01
    Scan the order

    Begin one session that can hold multiple orders and lines.

  2. 02
    Capture the material

    Scan part, batch, and serial number only when the profile requires it.

  3. 03
    Confirm the bin

    The workflow pauses at the operational decision that still needs human judgment.

  4. 04
    Review the result

    Automated issuing and transfer-order steps finish with an LT23 summary.

Control by design

Speed came from removing repetition—not removing judgment.

01Variable serial profiles

The workflow requests serial data only where it is applicable to the material.

02Quantity discipline

Structured capture reduces the risk of issuing the wrong quantity or overlooking a line.

03Human bin confirmation

Automation pauses where the associate must confirm the physical source location.

04Closed-loop confirmation

The process continues through transfer-order confirmation and returns a final summary.

Operational evidence

The strongest result was capacity under pressure.

After an SAP outage created a recovery backlog, the team processed approximately 200 repair-station orders—about 400 lines—the following day.

30–60 secestimated manual time per line when no error occurs
<10 secestimated guided time per line when no error occurs
Earlier capacitynormal order volume reached around midday, opening time for other work

Timing and operational outcomes are practitioner estimates and observations, not a controlled time study. They are presented with their original context rather than as guaranteed results.

Why this matters

A better interface became a training and resilience system.

New and cross-trained associates can follow one guided experience instead of memorizing a chain of transactions.

Experienced users can process multiple orders in one session without rebuilding the same transaction sequence.

The operation retains a manual path while gaining a repeatable tool for normal volume and recovery events.
