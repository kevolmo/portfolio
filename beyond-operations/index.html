Google data-governance strategy case study

This academic exercise was not commissioned by or affiliated with Google. It explored how a global data enterprise might strengthen privacy compliance, policy consistency, data discovery, auditability, and stakeholder trust through a governed catalog and operating model.

Case hypothesis

Make governed, discoverable data the foundation for compliance, trust, and faster decisions.

The work used public research and third-party scenarios to evaluate an Alation-centered data-defense strategy alongside existing Google cloud and reporting capabilities.

Privacy, antitrust, transparency, and regulatory exposure create a governance imperative.

01Situation analysis

Used PESTEL and SWOT to connect privacy, antitrust, advertising dependence, trust, technology, and regulatory pressure.

02Governance gap

Compared existing infrastructure with the capabilities needed for cataloging, stewardship, policy, discovery, and auditability.

03Solution design

Proposed a data-defense strategy using Alation alongside existing cloud infrastructure and Looker Studio reporting.

04Implementation

Structured the change into data strategy, catalog preparation, implementation, stewardship, training, and early wins.

05Decision economics

Compared a three-year cost scenario with quantified benefits while treating avoided liability as a separate risk consideration.

01 · DiscoverCatalog and classify

Make data assets searchable, understandable, traceable, and easier to evaluate for appropriate use.

02 · GovernPolicy and stewardship

Connect ownership, definitions, standards, permissions, lineage, and just-in-time guidance.

03 · MonitorCompliance and improvement

Track adoption, data quality, audit coverage, policy adherence, exceptions, and performance against the prior state.

Data-defense operating model

The technology only works when ownership and behavior move with it.

The implementation plan emphasized executive sponsorship, data stewards, training, early use cases, quick wins, policy guidance, and continuous measurement—not simply software installation.

Monitoring design

The solution was tied to questions decision-makers could revisit.

  1. 01

    Is governed data compliant with applicable privacy policies and regulations?

  2. 02

    Is sensitive data protected and are access, lineage, and use understandable?

  3. 03

    How does the proposed governance model perform compared with the prior state?
